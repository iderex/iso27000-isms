## 2. What it is about

This standard describes the resilience of an organisation, meaning the ability to
carry on under strain and to change while doing so. It names principles and
attributes by which that shows.

The first point is at the same time the biggest disappointment for a project:
resilience cannot be introduced. It is a property and not a product. Whoever runs
it as a project ends up with a project, a document and a closing slide, and the
property is untouched by all of it.

The second point is the difference from continuity, and it is the real
contribution of this document. Continuity restores what was. Resilience is about
the case where what was is no longer available and the organisation has to
change. Those are two different abilities, and the second cannot be planned by
scenario, because the case in question was not foreseen.

The third point is the usable use of this document: as a mirror. The attributes
it names get read against one's own house, and what comes out are uncomfortable
questions and not tasks. For instance whether bad news reaches leadership before
it is outside. Such questions are worth it, and they stand in no checklist.

The fourth point is the trap. Turning it into a maturity assessment with scores
is tempting and wrong. The attributes are not independent of each other and not
measurable; a score on them looks like a measurement and is not. A maturity
assessment elsewhere is something else and has its place.

The fifth point is where it belongs on the route: this document is a poor start.
A house without the two figures from the analysis reads here about attributes
while the ground is missing. Nothing is certified against this standard either.

What does not stand here is the wording, and neither do the principles and
attributes this standard lists, singly or in number. Whoever needs either opens a
licensed copy.

## 3. Whom it serves, and whom it does not

For a leadership that already has a management system for continuity and notices
that it does not answer the real question.

For anyone reviewing after an event they came through who wants to know more than
the cause.

For anyone who has to describe a culture without falling into advertising
language.

Not for whoever is to build a management system. That is
[ISO 22301](../iso-22301/en.md).

Not for whoever has to handle a situation the plan did not foresee. That is
[ISO 22361](../iso-22361/en.md).

Not for whoever is looking for a checklist. This standard is none, and making one
out of it is the mistake from section 2.

## 5. What a practitioner does with it

Read it as a mirror and not as a specification. One session, two hours, with
leadership present.

Then formulate three questions for your own house that came out of it, and write
them down. Three, not thirty.

Then find an example from your own operation for each question where it shows.
Without an example it stays an assertion about the culture.

Then decide whether anything follows. For one of the three questions something
usually follows, for the others not, and that is a usable result.

In running operation the repetition after an event stays. Exactly then the
answers are honest, and exactly then nobody has time for it, which is why the
date has to stand beforehand.

## 6. Where it stops against the neighbour

Against [ISO 22301](../iso-22301/en.md): there stands a management system with
requirements. Here stands a description without requirements.

Against [ISO 22361](../iso-22361/en.md): there the subject is handling a
situation. Here the subject is the condition an organisation goes into a
situation in.

Against [ISO 22317](../iso-22317/en.md): there things get measured and
calculated. Here they get described, and confusing the two produces the score
from section 2.

Against [ISO/IEC 27001](../iso-iec-27001/en.md): there stands a management system
for information security. Resilience is not a requirement in it.

Against [ISO/IEC 27004](../iso-iec-27004/en.md): there stands how a figure is
built. The attributes here do not serve as one.

## 7. Before and after

## 8. Walk-through: using the document as a mirror

This walk-through follows the pattern in
[tutorials/en.md](../../tutorials/en.md). The example is invented.

Take a hospital reviewing after a two-day loss of network connectivity. The plan
held, and something was still not right. The question is: what was it?

Step 1, set the session while the event is fresh. In this example two weeks
afterwards, two hours, with leadership and four people from the wards.

Step 2, form three questions out of the document. In this example: when did
leadership learn that it was serious? Who was allowed to decide on the second day
without asking? What did the ward do that stood in no plan?

Step 3, find an example for each question. In this example it comes out that
leadership learned after eleven hours, that a senior doctor made a decision she
was not responsible for and that was right, and that one ward kept a list of its
own that nobody knew about afterwards.

Step 4, decide what follows. In this example something follows from the first
question: a reporting threshold above which leadership learns at once. From the
other two nothing follows, and that is written down.

Step 5, do not form the score. In this example it is expressly noted that no
rating arises, because it would feign a measurement.

Step 6, write the boundary. In this example all three answers rest on the
recollections of six people two weeks afterwards. That is a weak ground and gets
a line in the risk register. The pattern stands in
[templates/registers/risk-register/en.md](../../templates/registers/risk-register/en.md).

What comes out of it: three questions with examples, a new reporting threshold,
two expressly consequence-free findings and a line in the register. What does not
come out of it: a more resilient house. That arises, if at all, out of such
sessions over years.

The assumptions of this example: an event come through, a leadership that takes
part, two weeks of distance. Whoever cannot get leadership to the table has the
real finding in step 1 and not in step 6.

## 12. As read

This chapter refers to ISO 22316:2017 as the edition in force. Its catalog entry
carries `confirmation: confirmed`, resting on two independent sources, and was
read on 2026-08-04. The entry carries no amendment. The command and its output
stand in the German half.

The catalog carries no German title under this designation, and the reason
stands there in the field `title_de_note`. No German title is formed here.

The clause and control numbers in sections 4 and 11 are checked against the tree
and not against a licensed copy. They come from the tables that sit in the tree
and carry a read date of their own, which the German half prints. The same
calculation over `mappings/external/cis-controls.csv` gives 47 rows and over
`mappings/external/bsi-it-grundschutz.csv` 72 rows, both with the same date. A
number appearing in none of those three tables does not stand in this chapter.

No clause number of ISO 22316 itself is named, and that is deliberate. A number
nobody looked up is worse than none: it looks checkable.

The principles and attributes this standard names do not stand here, neither
singly nor in number. Reproducing them would be an adopted list; the boundary in
`copyright/en.md` rules that out. The three questions in section 8 are formed for
that example and are not a reproduction.

This edition is from 2017 and so older than the numbering of today's control set.
The link in section 4 is therefore laid over the numbers of 2022 and not over
those of the edition.

That the attributes are not independent of each other and not measurable is a
judgement and not a finding from the document. It is carried here as a judgement,
because a score on that ground is regularly used in practice as if it were a
measurement.

That resilience cannot be introduced is a judgement as well. Not measured is
whether houses with such projects come through an event better than houses
without.

No product, no procedure and no supplier is recommended here.

No licensed copy was consulted for this chapter.

Whether a new edition has appeared since the date named is not said by this
chapter.
