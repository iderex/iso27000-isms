## 2. What it is about

This part deals with the work itself: what happens between the demand and the
handover.

The first point decides everything else. What is asked for is not the best
search but one that can be explained. Whoever can say, in a dispute, where they
searched, with which terms, what was dropped and why, and who decided it, stands
better than somebody with a more thorough procedure that nobody can give an
account of. So the log comes into being during the work and not afterwards.

The second point is the most expensive mistake: collecting everything because it
feels safe. It feels safe because nothing is missing. It is expensive because
every extra volume gets passed on into the step where people read, and that is
where nearly all the money sits. What is not excluded at the front is paid for
at the back.

The third point is everything that makes the volume smaller: periods, people,
duplicates, attachments appearing twenty times. Every one of those moves is
allowed and every one has to be explainable. Where a machine decides what gets
read and what does not, a sample belongs beside it, showing how much it misses.
Without that sample the saving is an assertion.

The fourth point is the conflict with running operations. Retention periods run,
deletion routines run, and the order from part 2 stops both. Those three pull in
different directions, and whoever does not resolve the conflict in writing
resolves it by accident.

The fifth point is the handover. What form the material arrives in, which
entries travel with it and what was taken out of it is agreed with the other
side before the work and not after. A form settled only at the end means part of
the work gets done twice.

What does not stand here is the wording. Whoever needs it opens a licensed copy.

## 3. Whom it serves, and whom it does not

For anyone with a demand in the house who has to organise the work.

For anyone deciding how much volume may fall away before the reading.

For anyone directing a provider who wants to know what log to ask of them.

Not for whoever is looking for the terms. That is
[part 1](../iso-iec-27050-1/en.md).

Not for whoever wants to settle responsibility. That is
[part 2](../iso-iec-27050-2/en.md).

Not as a substitute for legal advice. What has to go out in a given case and
what may be withheld is said neither by this standard nor by this chapter.

## 5. What a practitioner does with it

First settle what gets logged, and settle it before the searching starts. Place,
period, terms, number of hits, what was dropped and on what grounds, and who
decided it. Six entries, one form.

Then narrow before collecting. Which people, which period, which stores. Every
narrowing gets a sentence saying why it is admissible.

Then collect without altering. What was collected is not touched again; the work
runs on a copy.

Then shrink the volume with the moves from section 2 and take a sample of what
falls out. The sample is small and its result stands in the log.

Then people read, and whatever gets held back gets a reason from a short,
previously settled list. Where something is taken out of a document rather than
the document being held back whole,
[ISO/IEC 27038](../iso-iec-27038/en.md) holds.

Then hand over in the agreed form and keep a copy of what was handed over.
Without that copy it is later open what the other side received.

## 6. Where it stops against the neighbour

Against [part 1](../iso-iec-27050-1/en.md): there stand the terms.

Against [part 2](../iso-iec-27050-2/en.md): there stands who orders it and how
it is weighed. Here the work is done.

Against [part 4](../iso-iec-27050-4/en.md): there stands what the systems have
to be able to do beforehand. What they cannot do makes every step here dearer.

Against [ISO/IEC 27038](../iso-iec-27038/en.md): there stands the taking of
single passages out of a document. That is a step inside this procedure and not
a separate job beside it.

Against [ISO/IEC 27037](../iso-iec-27037/en.md): there the subject is a piece of
evidence after an incident. The care over collecting is related, the occasion
and the extent are not.

## 7. Before and after

## 8. Walk-through: running a search that stays explainable

This walk-through follows the pattern in
[tutorials/en.md](../../tutorials/en.md). The example is invented.

Take a hospital in dispute with a supplier. The material asked for covers the
rollout of a system over two years. Six people were involved. The question is:
how is the search run so that it holds up in the dispute?

Step 1, set up the form before searching. Six columns, as in section 5.

Step 2, narrow and give reasons. In this example: six people, because they were
named in the project, two years and three months, because the contract was
signed three months before the start, four stores, because the other seven had
nothing to do with the project. Three sentences, three reasons.

Step 3, collect and do not touch again. The copy gets a date and a place, and
from here the work runs on the copy.

Step 4, shrink. In this example duplicates and circulars fall away. Of what
falls out, fifty items are read, and the log says how many of them should have
stayed after all. If that number is greater than zero, the move gets changed and
not the report.

Step 5, read and hold back. The grounds are settled beforehand: entries about
patients with no bearing on the dispute, legal advice, entries about third
parties. Where only part of a document is affected, it is taken out under
[ISO/IEC 27038](../iso-iec-27038/en.md) rather than the whole document being
held back.

Step 6, write the boundary. In this example the on-call mobile phones were not
searched, because the house cannot do it. That sentence stands in the log and
the matter gets a line in the risk register. The pattern stands in
[templates/registers/risk-register/en.md](../../templates/registers/risk-register/en.md).

What comes out of it: a log with six columns, three reasoned narrowings, an
unaltered copy, a measured sample, a short list of grounds for holding back and
a line in the register. What does not come out of it: the certainty of having
found everything. There is none in this field, and a log claiming it is the
point of attack.

The assumptions of this example: six people, four stores, a house that searches
itself. Whoever commissions a provider asks the same six columns of them and
checks the sample from step 4 themselves.

## 10. Does it need a presentation, and for whom

For two of the five audiences yes, for three no. The answer stands
language-neutral in `meta.yaml` beside this file, with a reason per audience.

In short: practitioners need the sentence that an explainable search beats a
better one nobody can give an account of, and engineering needs the sentence
that collecting everything is the expensive mistake and that a machine dropping
material needs a sample beside it. For management, all staff and audit a no with
its reason stands in the same file.

This chapter refers to ISO/IEC 27050-3:2020 as the edition in force. Its catalog
entry carries `confirmation: confirmed`, resting on two independent sources, and
was read on 2026-08-04. It carries no amendment and names a superseded edition.
The command and its output stand in the German half.

The clause and control numbers in sections 4 and 11 are checked against the tree
and not against a licensed copy. They come from the tables that sit in the tree

number appearing in none of those three tables does not stand in this chapter.

No clause number of ISO/IEC 27050-3 itself is named, and that is deliberate. A
number nobody looked up is worse than none: it looks checkable.

The sections this part divides the work into do not stand here, neither by name
nor in number. Reproducing them would be an adopted structure; the boundary in
`copyright/en.md` rules that out. Section 5 orders by what has to stand first
for the log to come into being at all, and that order is this chapter's own.

That nearly all the money sits in the reading is a general observation about
such proceedings and is not taken from this standard. No share for it stands
here; a figure would be an assertion without a measurement.

Not measured is how much a machine drop misses in practice. The fifty items in
section 8 are an assumption of the example and not a requirement of this
standard.

What has to go out in a given case is a legal question, not treated here and not
looked up.

No product, no tool and no supplier is recommended here.

No licensed copy was consulted for this chapter.

Whether a new edition has appeared since the date named is not said by this
