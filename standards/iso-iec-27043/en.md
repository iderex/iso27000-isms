## 2. What it is about

This standard describes an investigation as a whole, from the time before the
incident to its closing. The three neighbouring standards fill in single pieces
of it.

The sentence at the centre of it is about the beginning. The work that makes an
investigation possible happens before there is anything to investigate.
Afterwards, only what was already there can be used: the record that was already
running, the authority that was already written, the place that was already
named. Skip that part and you later run an investigation on whatever happens to
be left, and chance then decides what comes out.

The second point is a decision that is almost always skipped: whether to
investigate at all. Not every incident deserves an investigation. One that
begins with no named purpose also has no condition under which it ends, and it
then ends because nobody has any time left. So before the first step comes a
sentence saying what is to be answered at the end and what the answer is needed
for.

The third point is the activities that do not stand in a row but run alongside
all the others. The log, the unbroken chain of custody for every piece of
material, the authority the work rests on. Put them into the sequence as a
section of their own and you have them in exactly one place, and the chain
breaks at the first place where it was not kept.

The fourth point is the closing. An investigation nobody ended is running. The
material stays where it is because no person says it is no longer needed, and
the retention becomes one for which there is no longer a reason. Closing is a
step with a date and a person, not the absence of further work.

The fifth point is the price of being general. This frame fits a criminal
matter, an employment matter and a dispute with a supplier. That is exactly why
it decides nothing in any of them. Whoever wants concrete answers finds the
order and the gaps here, and the answers in the chapters beside it.

What does not stand here is the wording. Whoever needs it opens a licensed copy.

## 3. Whom it serves, and whom it does not

For anyone setting up a procedure for investigations in the house who wants to
know which pieces there are at all.

For anyone who has to decide whether an incident gets investigated and so far
has no sentence to hang that on.

For anyone facing the question of what readiness has to be paid for in advance.

Not for whoever is to carry out a single step. The chapters on
[ISO/IEC 27037](../iso-iec-27037/en.md),
[ISO/IEC 27041](../iso-iec-27041/en.md) and
[ISO/IEC 27042](../iso-iec-27042/en.md) stand beside it for that.

Not for whoever wants to stop an incident. That is
[ISO/IEC 27035-2](../iso-iec-27035-2/en.md), and it runs alongside an
investigation.

Not as a substitute for a legal basis. Whether the house may investigate itself
and how far is said neither by this standard nor by this chapter.

## 5. What a practitioner does with it

First write down what is to be available when it matters. Which records are
running, how far they reach, who may release them, which device may be taken out
of service. That list is short and costs money, and both belong before
management together.

Then write the sentence on which the decision to investigate is taken. It holds
the purpose: for a decision about a person, for a report to a supervisory body,
for a claim against a supplier, for the improvement afterwards. No purpose, no
investigation.

Then settle who grants the authority, and in what form. One sentence with a date
and a signature is enough; nothing at all is not.

Then determine what runs alongside: the log, the chain of custody for every
piece of material, and the updating of the authority when the investigation
widens. Those three do not stand in the sequence but beside it.

Then determine how the closing happens. Who declares the matter ended, what
happens to the material, and when what was learned goes into the improvement.

In running operation a yearly review of the list from the first step stays. A
record falls away, a store moves, and readiness shrinks without anyone having
taken a decision about it.

## 6. Where it stops against the neighbour

Against [ISO/IEC 27037](../iso-iec-27037/en.md): there stands one piece of this
frame, the first response to the material.

Against [ISO/IEC 27041](../iso-iec-27041/en.md): there stands another piece, the
proof that the chosen route holds.

Against [ISO/IEC 27042](../iso-iec-27042/en.md): there stands the third piece,
the analysis and the interpretation.

Against [ISO/IEC 27035-1](../iso-iec-27035-1/en.md): there stands the handling
of an incident, meaning stopping it and getting back. An investigation runs
alongside and has a different purpose; where both fall to the same person,
stopping wins in case of doubt, and that belongs known.

Against [ISO/IEC 27050-1](../iso-iec-27050-1/en.md): there the occasion is a
duty to disclose in a proceeding and not an incident. The frame differs, the
care over the material does not.

## 8. Walk-through: writing down readiness for an investigation once

This walk-through follows the pattern in
[tutorials/en.md](../../tutorials/en.md). The example is invented.

Take a hospital with no investigation unit of its own. So far somebody is
commissioned when needed, and each time it turns out that a record is missing or
does not reach far enough back. The question is: what gets settled in advance so
that the next investigation does not fail on the material?

Step 1, write down the sources that exist, with their reach. In this example
there are seven, and the shortest reaches seven days. That figure is the real
statement of the list.

Step 2, ask per source which investigation it is meant to carry. Where the
answer is that seven days do not reach for the case in mind, the choice is due:
keep longer and pay for it, or write the boundary down.

Step 3, write the authority. One sentence on who may order an investigation, one
sentence on who countersigns when staff are affected.

Step 4, write the decision sentence. In this example: an investigation runs when
patient data may be affected, when a duty to report is in play, or when a claim
against a third party depends on it. Otherwise the incident is handled and not
investigated.

Step 5, settle the log that runs alongside. A form with date, person, action,
where the material sits. Whoever does not fill it in has not done it.

Step 6, write the boundary. In this example two of the seven sources stay at
seven days because longer retention is not paid for. That is a knowingly
accepted danger and gets a line in the risk register. The pattern stands in
[templates/registers/risk-register/en.md](../../templates/registers/risk-register/en.md).

What comes out of it: a list of sources with their reach, a decision per source,
a written authority, a sentence about the whether, a form and a line in the
register. What does not come out of it: an investigation unit of its own. This
house does not need one, and the frame does not ask for one.

The assumptions of this example: no staff of its own for investigations, seven
sources, a house holding patient data. Whoever has staff of their own has more
to write in step 3 and the same in step 2.

## 10. Does it need a presentation, and for whom

For two of the five audiences yes, for three no. The answer stands
language-neutral in `meta.yaml` beside this file, with a reason per audience.

In short: management needs the two sentences that the whether is a decision with
a price and that readiness is paid for in advance or not at all, and
practitioners need the sentence that the decisive work lies before the incident.
For engineering, all staff and audit a no with its reason stands in the same
file.

## 12. As read

This chapter refers to ISO/IEC 27043:2015 as the edition in force. Its catalog
entry carries `confirmation: unconfirmed`, resting on a single source, and was
read on 2026-08-04. While it stands unconfirmed, the edition given in this
chapter is only as good as that one source. The entry carries no amendment. The
command and its output stand in the German half.

The clause and control numbers in sections 4 and 11 are checked against the tree
and not against a licensed copy. They come from the tables that sit in the tree
and carry a read date of their own, which the German half prints. The same
calculation over `mappings/external/cis-controls.csv` gives 47 rows and over
`mappings/external/bsi-it-grundschutz.csv` 72 rows, both with the same date. A
number appearing in none of those three tables does not stand in this chapter.

No clause number of ISO/IEC 27043 itself is named, and that is deliberate. A
number nobody looked up is worse than none: it looks checkable.

The sections this standard divides an investigation into do not stand here,
neither by name nor in number, and neither does the term it gives the activities
running alongside. Reproducing either would be an adopted structure; the boundary
in `copyright/en.md` rules that out. Section 2 names five points in its own words
instead, and the order in section 5 follows what a house has to settle first.

This edition is from 2015 and so is older than the numbering of today's control
set. The link in section 4 is therefore laid over the 2022 numbers and not over
those of the edition.

That an investigation with no named purpose ends because nobody has any time
left, and that stopping wins where handling and investigating fall to the same
person, are general observations about running operations and are not taken from
this standard.

Not measured is how many houses keep such a list. The seven sources and the
seven days in section 8 are assumptions of the example and not a survey.

No product, no tool and no supplier is recommended here.

No licensed copy was consulted for this chapter.

Whether a new edition has appeared since the date named is not said by this
chapter.
