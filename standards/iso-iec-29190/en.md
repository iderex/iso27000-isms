## 2. What it is about

This document deals with judging how far an organisation has come in privacy.

The first point is the purpose. Such a judgement is a means of holding a
conversation about the state and not a result to be hung on a wall. Its value
lies in what comes up while it is being collected. Anyone reading this chapter
for one sentence only reads that one.

The second point is the metric trap. As soon as a level is a target, it gets
reached rather than earned. Anyone taking a rating into an objectives agreement
gets a better figure next year and does not know whether anything has changed.

The third point is evidence. A claimed level with no evidence is a self-estimate
with a figure in front of it. The question of what shows it is more laborious
than the question of the level and the only one that carries.

The fourth point is comparison. The useful comparison is with yourself over time.
Comparison with other houses presupposes that everybody measured the same thing,
and that is rarely the case.

The fifth point is age. This edition is from 2015. The shape of privacy work has
changed since, and what gets judged here has to be held against today's shape.

What does not stand here is the wording. Anyone who needs it looks it up in a
licensed copy.

## 3. For whom, and for whom not

For anyone who has to describe the state of privacy work in their own house.

For anyone who has to justify an order for improvements.

For anyone reading a rating presented to them who wants to know what to check
about it.

Not for anyone looking for requirements on a management system. That is
[ISO/IEC 27701](../iso-iec-27701/en.md).

Not for anyone wanting to assess a single processing operation. That is
[ISO/IEC 29134](../iso-iec-29134/en.md).

Not as evidence outwards. A maturity rating is not a certificate.

## 5. What you do with it

You determine what gets judged before judging. Without that step every
participant judges something different.

Then you collect the state, and in such a way that every statement carries a
piece of evidence. A conversation with no evidence is an opinion.

Then you write down what is missing to reach the next level. That is the part
that creates work, and therefore the part readily left out.

Then you decide what of it gets taken on, and what expressly does not. A rating
without that decision is a report.

Then you fix the next date and use the same yardstick next time. A changed
yardstick makes the course unreadable.

In operation what remains is restraint. The figure does not belong in the
objectives agreement, and anyone taking it there loses the information.

## 6. Boundary against the neighbouring standard

Against [ISO/IEC 27701](../iso-iec-27701/en.md): there stand requirements that
are met or not. Here stands a description of the state in levels, and the two
answer different questions.

Against [ISO/IEC 29134](../iso-iec-29134/en.md): there a processing operation
gets assessed. Here the organisation gets assessed.

Against [ISO/IEC 27004](../iso-iec-27004/en.md): there the subject is
measurement and evaluation in information security. The traps are the same, and
this chapter does not repeat them.

Against a certificate: a level is not a certification and gets confirmed by
nobody outside the house as long as nobody checks it.

## 7. Precondition and what follows

Presupposed is running privacy work. Anyone with nothing yet measures a zero and
learns little from it.

Presupposed is a willingness to demand evidence, including for one's own
statements.

Presupposed is a place that decides what follows from the result.

What follows is the order of improvements and the repetition with the same
yardstick.

## 8. Walk-through: evidencing a level instead of claiming it

This walk-through follows the pattern in
[tutorials/en.md](../../tutorials/en.md). The example is invented.

Assume a clinic judging its privacy work for the second time. For handling
requests for information a middle level gets claimed, on the ground that a
procedure exists. The question is: does that claim carry?

Step 1, turn the claim into a question about evidence. Not is there a procedure,
but how many requests came in last year, how long did answering take, and where
does that stand.

Step 2, fetch the evidence. In the example four requests are found, three
answered within a week, one after eleven weeks. For the fourth there is no record
of why.

Step 3, measure the level against it. A procedure that did not carry in one of
four cases and does not know the reason is not at the claimed level. That finding
is uncomfortable and is the step's yield.

Step 4, name the difference. What is missing is not the procedure but the record
that makes an outlier explainable.

Step 5, decide. Either the record comes, or the level gets carried lower. Both
are permissible; only both at once is not.

Step 6, fix the yardstick. Next time the same question gets asked, or the course
is not one.

Step 7, take the boundary into the register. Where a level gets carried lower
than wanted, a line goes into the risk register following
[templates/registers/risk-register/en.md](../../templates/registers/risk-register/en.md).

What comes out of it: a level evidenced or refuted, a named difference, a
decision, a fixed yardstick and a line in the register. What does not come out of
it: a figure for management that means something on its own.

The assumptions of this example: a second judgement, one subject, four requests.
Anyone judging for the first time has no course and keeps everything else.

## 10. Does this subject need a presentation

For three of the five audiences yes, for two no. The answer stands
language-neutrally in `meta.yaml` beside this file, with a reason per audience.

In short: management needs the sentence about the metric trap, because it is
management that can turn a figure into a target. Practitioners need the
comparison with themselves and the question of evidence. Auditors look for the
evidence behind every level.
