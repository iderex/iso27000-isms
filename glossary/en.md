# Glossary

## 1. What this glossary is and is not

It replaces no definition. It explains in our own words what a term means when
it turns up in the learning path and in the chapters of this repository, and it
shows the way to the place where the binding version stands.

The difference is not a formality. Anyone arguing from a term in an audit, in a
contract or in a certification argues from the binding version and not from an
explanation. This file gets a learner to where they find it, and it does not
step into its place.

The German version stands in [de.md](de.md). The machine-readable term list
arises separately as a CSV and does not repeat this text.

## 2. Where the binding definition stands

The vocabulary part of the 27000 series stood in ISO/IEC 27000. The edition
this repository's catalog carries as current is the one from 2026, and it is
recorded there under a designation that no longer names the vocabulary; the
entry sits in `catalog/entries/core-27000.csv` with its two sources and the date
of the reading. Anyone holding an older edition finds the terms there in
clause 3.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 does not define the terms itself. Its clause 3 points to
ISO/IEC 27000 for them. An entry here pointing at a term from the vocabulary
therefore points there and not into a clause of 27001.

The terms are freely reachable through ISO's Online Browsing Platform, at
`https://www.iso.org/obp`. They can be looked up there without buying an
edition. That is the place every entry here means when it refers to the
vocabulary.

Where a term is instead required or named in a clause of ISO/IEC 27001:2022,
the entry names that clause. How those clause numbers were checked stands
openly beside them: against several public secondary sources that agree on
them, and not against a licensed copy. Looking it up stays the business of
whoever has one.

## 3. How an entry is built

Three parts, always in this order.

The explanation in our own words. No quotation, no copied definition and no
paraphrase that follows the wording of the original.

One sentence on where in the learning path the term is used. A term without
that sentence does not belong here, because this glossary does not collect, it
carries the path.

The place where the binding version stands, by standard, clause and edition.
Where there is none, that stands there.

## 4. The terms

### 4.1 Asset

Anything that has value for an organisation and is therefore to be protected.
That is not only devices and programs but also data, contracts, rooms,
deliveries and the knowledge of individual people. The term is deliberately
wide, because protection that only thinks of devices walks past the first paper
file.

It is used from step 2 on, once an asset register is kept and a risk assessment
is hung off it.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.2 Audit, internal

A planned examination of one's own organisation by one's own organisation, to
establish whether the ISMS meets its own arrangements and the requirements of
the standard, and whether it actually runs. It is carried out by people who do
not themselves answer for the area under examination.

It is used on step 2, where operating and checking are covered.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 9.2.

### 4.3 Threat

A circumstance that can harm an asset. A threat is not yet damage and not yet a
risk; it only becomes a risk when a vulnerability comes with it that the threat
can act through.

It is used on step 1, in the risk assessment.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.4 Documented information

Everything an organisation has to write down and keep so that its ISMS can be
followed. The term gathers two things that used to have separate names: what is
meant to hold, so policies and procedures, and what actually happened, so
records.

It is used on step 1 and again on step 2, because almost every requirement asks
for a record at its end.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 7.5.

### 4.5 Statement of applicability

The compilation saying, for every control of the annex, whether it is applied
and why, and giving the reason where one is not. It is not a tick sheet but the
result of the risk treatment, held against the annex.

It is used on step 1, at the end of the risk work, and it is the result a
certification takes hold of first.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.3.

### 4.6 Scope

The determination of which parts of an organisation, which sites, which
activities and which systems belong to the ISMS. What lies outside does not
disappear, but it is not steered by this management system, and the interfaces
to it belong expressly inside.

It is used on step 1 as the very first decision and again on step 3, where the
reader's own context decides how it is cut.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 4.3.

### 4.7 Information security

The protection of information in every form, not only the digital kind. What
the protection is measured against are the three objectives in section 4.19.

The term is used on step 0 first and everywhere after that.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.8 Information security management system

The whole of the arrangements, responsibilities, procedures and records with
which an organisation steers its information security. It is not a tool and not
a piece of software. The decisive part of the phrase is management system: what
gets steered is what gets measured, evaluated and changed.

It is used from step 0 on and is the subject of step 1.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
requirements on such a system stand in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, clauses 4 to 10.

### 4.9 Interested party

Anyone the ISMS concerns or who influences it. Customers, staff, supervisory
authorities, suppliers, insurers, in some cases the public. The term stands at
the beginning because the requirements on the ISMS arise out of what these
parties expect.

It is used on step 1 when the scope is cut and on step 3 for the reader's own
context.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
determination is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 4.2.

### 4.10 Corrective action and nonconformity

A nonconformity is the finding that a requirement is not met. A corrective
action is not the repair of the single case but the removal of its cause, so
that the same case does not come back. That distinction is the whole content of
the term.

It is used on step 2, after the internal audit.

Both are required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 10.2.

### 4.11 Management review

The regular occupation of top management with the ISMS, in which it looks at
what the measurements, the audits and the incidents produced, and derives
decisions from them. It is the point at which information security becomes a
task of the leadership rather than staying the task of one office in the house.

It is used on step 2, at the end of operating and checking.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 9.3.

### 4.12 Control

Something that changes a risk. That can be a piece of technology, and equally a
rule, a responsibility, a training or a contract. The commonest mistake at the
start is to equate control with technology.

The term is used on step 1, in the risk treatment, and it is the subject of
ISO/IEC 27002.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
controls themselves stand in ISO/IEC 27002:2022 and in the annex of
ISO/IEC 27001:2022, each with its number.

### 4.13 Maturity

An estimate of how reliably and repeatably an activity is carried out, usually
on a scale of a few steps. The term does not come from ISO/IEC 27001 and is not
required there; it is a tool for one's own steering.

It is used on step 2, where the maturity assessment sits beside it as a
template.

There is no binding version in the 27000 series. Anyone using a scale fixes it
themselves and writes it down, because otherwise the same step means something
else in two years.

### 4.14 Residual risk

What is left of a risk once the controls take effect. It is never zero. Anyone
who holds it to be zero has either overestimated the controls or not understood
the risk, and it gets approved expressly rather than accepted in silence.

It is used on step 1, at the end of the risk treatment.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
approval is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.3.

### 4.15 Risk

The possibility that something happens which affects the objectives of the
organisation, together with how large that effect is. In information security
an unwanted effect is almost always what is meant, even though the general
notion of risk knows both directions.

It is used from step 0 on and is the subject of step 1.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.16 Risk assessment

Establishing which risks exist, how large they are and which get treated first.
It stands before the choice of controls and not after it. Anyone choosing
controls first and looking for the risks afterwards has worked through a list
and not made an assessment.

It is used on step 1 and is the subject of ISO/IEC 27005.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.2, and its performance in 8.2.

### 4.17 Risk treatment

The decision about what happens to an assessed risk: reduce it, share it, avoid
it or carry it knowingly. The controls follow from that decision, and only
afterwards is the annex used to check whether something was forgotten.

It is used on step 1, immediately after the risk assessment.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.3, and its implementation in 8.3.

### 4.18 Risk owner

The person responsible for a particular risk and deciding how it gets treated.
Not the person implementing the control, but the one who has to answer for the
consequences.

It is used on step 1, because a risk list without responsibility belongs to
nobody after a short while.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
role is named in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.2 and 6.1.3.

### 4.19 The objectives: confidentiality, integrity, availability

The three properties information security is measured against. Confidentiality
means only those have access who are meant to. Integrity means a piece of
information is unaltered and complete, or that an alteration at least becomes
detectable. Availability means it is there when it is needed.

They are used on step 0 and in every risk assessment after that, because damage
always shows up at one of the three.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.20 Vulnerability

A property of an asset or of a control that a threat can act through. A
vulnerability with no matching threat does no damage, and a threat with no
matching vulnerability does none either; only the two together make a risk.

It is used on step 1, in the risk assessment.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.21 Security event and security incident

An event is an observation that might matter for security. An incident is an
event, or a sequence of them, where that is likely and which therefore has to
be handled. The distinction decides what triggers a report and what does not.

It is used on step 2, in operating.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2.

### 4.22 Continual improvement

The obligation to keep making the ISMS more suitable, more adequate and more
effective, including at times when nothing is broken. It is the counterpart to
the corrective action: one reacts, the other does not.

It is used on step 2, at the end, and it is the reason an ISMS is not a project
with a closing date.

It is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 10.1.

### 4.23 Monitoring and measurement

Monitoring means establishing what state something is in. Measuring means
assigning it a value. Anyone who only monitors notices a change; anyone who
measures can compare it over time.

Both are used on step 2 and are the subject of ISO/IEC 27004.

Both are required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 9.1.

### 4.24 Effectiveness

The extent to which what was planned is actually reached. A control can be
implemented and still be ineffective, and that difference is exactly the one an
audit looks for.

The term is used on step 2, because without it measuring is only counting.

The binding version stands in the vocabulary of the series, see section 2. The
evaluation of effectiveness is required in ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 9.1.

### 4.25 Certification and accreditation

An organisation gets certified, by a certification body confirming that its
ISMS meets the requirements. The certification body itself gets accredited, by
an accreditation body confirming that it may and can do that. A certificate
from a body that is not accredited therefore says something different from one
from a body that is.

The distinction is used on step 2 and again on step 4, where the standards for
the certification bodies themselves come up.

There is no definition of these in ISO/IEC 27001. The requirements on
certification bodies stand in ISO/IEC 27006, whose entries sit in the catalog
under `catalog/entries/core-27000.csv`.

## 5. What is deliberately missing

This glossary is not complete and is not meant to become complete. The
vocabulary of the series carries far more terms than stand here, and a glossary
rebuilding it entry by entry is an adoption, even where every single
explanation is worded on its own. This repository's checklist names exactly
that case.

The line drawn here: a term is taken in when the learning path needs it and a
beginner cannot follow a step without it. A term is left out when it only turns
up in a specialist standard, and equally when it means the same thing in
everyday language as it does in the standard.

One property follows from that, and it is not a defect: anyone not finding a
term here is not at the end but at the place where they have to look into the
vocabulary of the series. Section 2 says where that is freely reachable.

## 6. What this file is not

No check enforces it. Nothing in this repository refuses a chapter that uses a
term differently from here, and nothing notices a missing entry. What is
checked mechanically stands in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md),
section 20.

It is not legal information and not consulting either. What a term means in a
contract, in a supervisory requirement or in an audit is decided there and not
here.
