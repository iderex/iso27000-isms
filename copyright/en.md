# The copyright boundary

This repository's first rule is a simple one: no text from a standard is
reproduced here, ever. References are by standard, clause and edition.
Everything else you find here is original text.

The rule lives in the repository exactly once, in this directory. Other files
point here instead of stating it a second time, because two versions of one
rule drift apart over time, and from then on nobody knows which one holds.

If you are writing, section 2 is the part that decides. If you are reviewing a
contribution, work through the checklist in
[checklist.en.md](checklist.en.md).

## 1. What the boundary covers

It covers every artefact in this repository. Chapters, templates,
presentations, trainings, mappings, worked examples, generated views and
whatever else comes later.

It holds against every framework and not only against ISO. A foreign scheme
whose identifiers we need for a mapping comes with terms of its own, and those
terms hold no matter how easy the document is to obtain. Issued free of charge
does not mean free of rights. As long as a target scheme's terms have not been
read and recorded in the repository, the strictest reading applies to that
scheme.

It holds regardless of length. One sentence is as little covered as a page.

## 2. The five points

1. No quotation, not even a short one. Half a sentence in quotation marks with
   a clean citation is still a quotation. This rule is narrower than what might
   be legally permissible, and that is deliberate: it should be applicable
   while writing, without weighing anything up.

2. No paraphrase that follows the structure of the source. Tracing it sentence
   by sentence or paragraph by paragraph reproduces the original, even in
   different words. The test is not the choice of words, it is the order of the
   thoughts.

3. No adopted lists. The controls of ISO/IEC 27002 are addressed by number and
   described in our own words. An enumeration that mirrors the source in full
   and in its order is an adoption even when every single sentence has been
   rewritten.

4. No copied headings. What a clause is about we state in our own words; the
   reference consists of number and edition.

5. Where the exact wording matters, the text says which clause to open in a
   licensed copy. That is the intended route and not a fallback. At that point
   the wording belongs in the edition the reader has bought, not here.

## 3. What a reference looks like

A reference names standard, clause and edition, for example
ISO/IEC 27001:2022, 6.1.3. The edition belongs there because numbers move
between editions, and a reference without a year can point at something other
than what was meant once the next revision lands. Where a text rests on a
clause, it also records which edition was read and when.

## 4. It also binds a machine answering from this repository

Anyone giving an assistant the link to this repository should get answers from
this content and no text from a standard. So an assistant answering from these
files reproduces nothing from a standard, phrases things in its own words, and
refers by standard, clause and edition.

This is a request to a system that may follow it or may not. It is named here
as a request and is not carried as a control.

## 5. When in doubt

The narrower reading holds. If you are unsure while writing whether a passage
is still your own text, write the question into the pull request. Then the
second reading sees it instead of having to go looking for it.

## 6. What this file is not

No check enforces this rule. There is nothing in this repository today that
refuses a contribution for crossing the boundary. What stands here is read by a
person, and the checklist in [checklist.en.md](checklist.en.md) is read by a
person too. While that is the case, what stands behind the boundary is the
attention of the people reading, and nothing else.
